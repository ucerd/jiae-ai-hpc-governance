# Operational governance and management clarifications

Source: centre management clarifications on 26 November 2026 during manuscript revision. The three CSVs in `data/management_clarifications/` record attributed summary information. They are not a transcript, an independently assessed sample, or direct extracts of the original agreements, recordings or financial ledger. The historical reporting window is unchanged. Retrospective applicability to every case has not been established.

## Project intake and gate decisions
A Project Initial Agreement (PIA) aligns the end user and development team at TRL 1–3: problem definition, structured data preparation, and feasibility/PoC. Around TRL 3, a subsequent agreement or MoU defines development deliverables and responsibilities through TRL 6. Management describes joint reviews of milestone deliverables, updated agreements and end-user confirmation before progression; funding and applicable regulatory requirements may constrain advancement. This provides external partner input but is not an independent audit of retrospective research coding.

Gate criteria are use-case specific. For example, classification projects may specify held-out accuracy, sensitivity or F1-score; software services may specify throughput, concurrent users, latency and reliability. Management reports that TRL 6 involves relevant industrial standards and sustained validation (two to three days where appropriate), and applicable regulatory requirements. A chatbot is an illustrative use case with client-specific load, duration, data rate and compute constraints; it is not a reported benchmark. The gate should retain measured results and approval evidence. No universal numeric thresholds or regulatory approvals are inferred.

## Iterative AI life cycle
The local mapping distinguishes readiness from the implementation sequence. As design extensions, dataset/model versioning, drift monitoring, retraining triggers, rollback and revalidation allow a gate to be reopened when requirements or operating conditions change. These extensions are not asserted to have been implemented consistently across all historical cases.

## HPC and financing
Management attributes demand to broader access, training, software maturation, industrial relationships and longer modelling, simulation and AI workloads. The historical user/job counts are compatible with expanded service activity; they cannot isolate automated batch processing or causes. The mixed CPU/GPU accounting series is not a measure of physical utilization.

Management reports approximate 2025 cost coverage of 70% from income-generating activities and 30% from NEF support. Industrial clients fund development delivery and associated developer payments; NEF support includes shared operating costs and research salaries. Finance reconciliation and auditable records are reported to exist, but neither the ledger nor the precise accounting denominator has been inspected. Conflicting verbally reported monetary figures are not included. This is partial cost recovery, not verified break-even, audited profitability or a seven-year series. Restricted research awards and in-kind contributions are kept separate from operating income. The financing CSV records the two reported shares without deriving or verifying them.
